# Notes on CPT Coding for 3D-Printed Anatomic Guides and Models

> **Read the [Disclaimer](#disclaimer-no-warranty-not-advice) at the end of this document before using any of the information below.**

## Scope

These notes describe the U.S. CPT Category III codes most commonly associated with the kind of outputs that a virtual surgical planning (VSP) workflow can help *design* — namely patient-specific 3D-printed surgical cutting/drilling guides, and, where applicable, a 3D-printed anatomic model (for example a neomandible model used for plate pre-bending).

They are coding *reference notes only*. They do not describe, endorse, or certify any specific software, and they do not establish that any particular output is reimbursable.

## The Category III CPT codes most often relevant

CPT is a registered trademark of, and the code set is owned and maintained by, the American Medical Association (AMA). The following is a paraphrased, plain-language summary of the relevant code families; it is **not** the official AMA descriptor text. Always consult the current official CPT codebook for the authoritative language.

**Anatomic guides (cutting / drilling guides):**

- **0561T** — production of the *first* individually designed and 3D-printed patient-specific anatomic guide, derived from a patient's image data set(s).
- **0562T** — production of *each additional* such guide. This is reported as an add-on to 0561T (i.e., it is used in addition to, not instead of, the first-guide code).

**Anatomic models (for context, in case a printed model is also produced):**

- **0559T** — production of the *first* individually prepared and processed 3D-printed anatomic model component derived from a patient's image data set(s).
- **0560T** — production of *each additional* such model component. Reported as an add-on to 0559T.

A practical note on counting: the add-on codes (0560T, 0562T) are driven by the number of distinct guides/model components produced, so an accurate record of how many separate items were designed and printed matters for correct reporting.

These codes describe the **physical 3D-printed object**. They are distinct from image post-processing / 3D rendering codes (e.g., the 76376 / 76377 family), which cover the segmentation and reconstruction work rather than the printed item. Reporting both for the same work is generally not appropriate; check current coding guidance.

## What "Category III" means for reimbursement

Category III codes are temporary codes used to track emerging technologies, services, and procedures. Several consequences follow from this status:

- They carry **no assigned relative value units (RVUs)** and **no national fee-schedule amount**. Any payment is determined at the discretion of the payer or, for Medicare, priced by the local contractor.
- Some payers treat 3D-printed guides/models as **investigational** or **bundle them into the primary procedure**, meaning no separate payment is made even on a clean claim.
- Coverage and payment therefore depend heavily on **the specific payer's policy, prior authorization, and the strength of the supporting documentation** — not on the code alone.

## Practical documentation considerations

Programs that collect on these codes typically maintain a documented "thread" tying the printed item to the clinical decision. Commonly cited elements include: a physician order establishing medical necessity; a clinical indication linking the guide/model to surgical planning; a separate dictated report describing the item and its components; a record of the fabrication process and materials; and an accurate count of guides/model components (relevant to the add-on codes). Verifying the payer's policy and obtaining prior authorization **before** the service is generally more effective than billing first and appealing later.
